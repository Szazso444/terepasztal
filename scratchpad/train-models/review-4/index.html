Terepasztal Train Models 4

The fourth pilot, after your notes on the third. Every picture is a game render at the identical simulation pose: the current look, the previous pilot and this one side by side. Mark each subject and question; your choices are saved here and I read them back.

  • One rule behind both fixes: pivots where the prototype has its running gear. Until now every medium body hung on pivots at a fixed 70% of its length, and the trucks were drawn elsewhere to look right. That is what drifted (pilot 2) and splayed (pilot 3). Now each vehicle's pivots are put where its image has the trucks (pivots in src/data), so what is drawn is what carries the body. Which track each train may use is measured from the geometry and did not change for any of them.
  • Flying Scotsman: the frame stands on its coupled wheels. As on a real or a model locomotive, the three coupled axles are rigid in the frame, so the frame is placed on the rail where they are and along the rail there (coupled in src/data); the leading bogie and the trailing axle swivel and slide sideways under it. Pilot 3 turned the coupled wheels with the rail instead: on the tightest curves they sat 0.17 tiles off the boiler line at 12.5°. Now they stay within 0.05 tiles of the rail, square under the boiler. The cost: on those curves the cab end swings outward and the trailing axle slides out from under the cab.
  • Two more steam engines with the same rule, so the choice is not made on one engine: the LMS Black Five (4-6-0, no trailing axle) and the BR 9F (2-10-0, the longest coupled wheelbase in the roster, with a pony truck in front).
  • F7: trucks where the image has them, turning with their end gear. The pilot-2 positions are back, now as the game's pivots, so the body rests on them through the switch. The pilot, snowplow, coupler and steps turn with their truck (your rule for rigid bodies on two or more trucks). Pilot 3 only moved the lower half of the pilot, so the upper half stayed on the body over it; now the whole piece goes. The missing wheel was the front truck sitting under the pilot's plate.
  • Hidden sides no longer plain brown. The side the image hides is rebuilt as the mirror of the seen one, shape and colour, so the trucks have wheels on both sides. Faces no image shows, such as the F7's rear end or a truck's top, take the colours of the nearest seen surface of the same piece: the rear end is now red with the stripe carried round. The pipeline also takes a second image per vehicle (see below).
  • SD40-2 follows the same rule: trucks at the image's positions, pilots and steps on the trucks.

Decisions for you

Parametric running gear · every heading on its rails

Bogies

Bogies are rendered per train, in the same run as the body. The F7's and SD40-2's come from their own reconstructions, mirrored to both sides, with their pilots, couplers and steps. The steam engines' leading bogies, trailing axle and pony truck are built round at the wheels measured on their images. Each sheet shows all 48 headings with the game's rail centres under it; current is the generic truck the game draws without them.

Flying Scotsman on the tightest curve (radius 0.5 tile)

Why the wheels splayed, measured

What would help most

A rear view of each locomotive

Yes, please send more pictures. The side the studio image hides is now filled by mirroring the seen side, so a picture of the other side adds little. What no picture shows yet is the back: the F7's rear end, a tender's back, a cab's rear wall. The pipeline now takes a second image per vehicle, finds its camera by fitting the model's outline to it (tested on the F7: outline match 0.95), matches its colours to the first image and paints what only it sees.

  • A rear three-quarter view from either side, in the same style and lighting as the studio image, whole vehicle in frame, on a transparent background.
  • Save it next to the studio image as assets/source/base-v1/loco-<name>-rear.png (for example loco-f7-rear.png); the pipeline picks it up by that name.
