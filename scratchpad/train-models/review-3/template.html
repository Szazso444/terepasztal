The third pilot, after your notes on the second. Every picture is a game render at the identical simulation pose: the current look, the previous pilot and this one side by side. Mark each subject and question; your choices are saved here and I read them back.

  • Flying Scotsman drivers where you drew them. The coupled wheels are a bogie again, on the rail at their place in the image (your blue line), with the rail's own angle there. They use the rear pivot's bogie slot, drawn 0.42 tiles ahead of it with bogieDraw, which places a sprite on the track rather than beside it. The leading bogie hangs at its pivot.
  • F7 back on the game's mechanism. Its trucks sit on the simulation's pivots again (no inboard offset), so the body rests on them through the switch and curves. Each truck taken from the model is also squared up (up to 1.1° of pitch and 0.7° of yaw corrected), so the trucks run parallel to the rails like the body.
  • End gear researched per prototype. On the F7 and SD40-2 the pilot, snowplow, coupler and steps hang from the body underframe; their trucks carry nothing. So they stay on the body; the F7 tab Gear on trucks shows your variant at the same poses to compare. Where a prototype hangs them on its running gear (Crocodile, GG1, Garratt, Big Boy front unit), they will turn with that bogie.
  • SD40-2: three tiles, two trucks. size: large, bogies: 2 in src/data, so it runs on high-speed track only (in-cab signalling fitted); filmed on a high-speed loop. Its own trucks sit at the large-body pivots, closer to the middle.
