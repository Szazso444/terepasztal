Hills, rails and climbing trains

All images are the game's own renderer (review scene, seed of the terrain-production map, 1440×900). Click an image for full size. Locked: terraces at 1/4 tile-side levels with rock banks.

  • In the game now: rails no longer flatten hills. A straight track runs on its own bed that climbs from edge to edge; curves, switches, stations and buildings need a tile no bank reaches into.
  • In the game now: trains run at 50% speed on a tile that climbs and 120% on one that descends.
  • In the game now: the tile tooltip shows elevation, 2 m per level (a level is a quarter of the 8 m tile side). Sea level and flat ground read "At sea level", hills reach 4 m, mountains 8 m.
  • In the game now: rock only on stacked banks (two levels within about a tile, and all mountain banks); a single 1/4 bank stays grass with an odd outcrop. Boulders scatter along stacked banks.
  • In the game now: the peak sprites fade into the rock over a band twice as tall.
  • Preview only, your call: cast shadow, height tint, soil skirts.

3 · Rails on a hill

One straight line, laid with the game's builder

16 straight tiles running east at row 71, from open ground over a hill. Levels along the line:

1112222221

Each rail tile climbs from the mean level of its west edge to the mean of its east edge, so pieces meet at every edge. The ramp is spread over the tiles on both sides of a bank: half a level each. Where a flat tile sits between two ramps (a one-tile level-1 plateau) the rail shows a slight bend at the joint.

4 · Trains on the climb

Small, medium and large at 1/4 and 1/8

Locomotive head on the first level-2 tile, heading uphill, consist trailing down the bank. Each body and bogie stands on the rail under it and pitches along its own heading. Current sprites (the illustrated models from the local session are not in yet).

Grade on a ramp tile: 1/4 → half a level (1 m) over 8 m = 12.5% · 1/8 → 0.5 m over 8 m = 6.25%. Speed factor is the same at both: 0.5 up, 1.2 down.
