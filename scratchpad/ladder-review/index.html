Terepasztal Size Ladders

Every locomotive is now the asset pipeline's own model: image to 3D model, then rendered in Blender at each length, cut into the same parts the game moves. Side by side on straight track and through its first curve, at today's lengths, on Ladder A (0.5 to 3 tiles) and on Ladder B (0.5 to 4).

  • The models: 14 engines already had one; the other 25 went through the pipeline today with the same settings, without the hand-measured wheel positions the pilot engines got. Some are rough, but they show size and proportion. Each keeps its own wheels for this test, so the game draws no bogies under them.
  • Today's lengths are the game's 1, 2 and 3 tiles with these same models, so only the length changes from picture to picture. The 3-tile engines run on 2×2 curves, the rest on today's 1×1.
  • Ladder A and B use the rail spike's 2×2 regular curve; narrow engines stand on regular rails on the straight and use a 1×1 curve as a stand-in. Wagons are still the game's 1-tile boxes (question 2).
  • Applied: the Crocodile is a Battleship, the General and Jupiter stay Destroyers. Nothing in the game or the track has changed; this is a scratch branch.

My proposal for the 0.5 to 4 scale

Ladder B: Corvette 0.5, Frigate 1, Destroyer 1.5, Cruiser 2, Battleship 3, Juggernaut 4 tiles.

  • The four lower classes stay exactly as on Ladder A, so 30 of 39 engines keep the length you are testing.
  • The top two grow by a full tile each step instead of half a tile, so a Battleship looks clearly bigger than a Cruiser and a Juggernaut is twice a Cruiser's length.
  • The cost is overhang. On a 2×2 curve a 3-tile Battleship is 0.15 to 0.35 off the rail and a 4-tile Juggernaut 0.46 to 0.54. On a 3×3 curve that drops to 0.10 to 0.26 and 0.35 to 0.40; on 4×4 the Juggernauts are at 0.28 to 0.31.
  • Length kept is how long each engine is drawn against its own model: under 100 % it is squeezed, over 100 % stretched. The four lower classes come out at 62 to 112 %. The top two are at 64 to 100 % on Ladder A and 77 to 120 % on Ladder B. On B the Juggernauts sit almost at their models' own length, but PRR GG1 (120 %), SBB Ce 6/8 Crocodile (115 %), MÁV 424 Bivaly (114 %), BR 9F (105 %) would be stretched past theirs at 3 tiles.
  • So B works if the two top classes run on bigger curves. Main already keeps its biggest engines off regular track, so they could stay on high-speed track with 3×3 or bigger curves. Curve sizes are the rail chat's decision, and I haven't changed any track.

I also tried even steps (0.5, 1, 1.75, 2.5, 3.25, 4) and a 2-tile Destroyer (0.5, 1, 2, 2.5, 3, 4). Both change lengths you are already testing and add overhang lower down, where every engine runs on 2×2 curves. B leaves those classes alone.

Still open

I haven't decided any of these.

1Ladder A or Ladder B?

The side-by-side pictures above show the difference: the two ladders are the same up to Cruiser, then B gives each of the top two classes a full extra tile. B's cost is overhang on the 2×2 curve: Battleships 0.15 to 0.35, Juggernauts 0.46 to 0.54, unless those classes stay on bigger curves.

2Wagon lengths on the new scale

Today 18 freight wagons are 1 tile and the two coaches (Steel Coach, Pullman Car) are 2 tiles. Every render on this page keeps them that way. On either ladder a 2-tile coach is as long as a Cruiser. Should wagons get the same ladder (a few fixed lengths per wagon type), or stay at 1 and 2? Tell me what you prefer and I'll render it next to today's wagons before changing anything.

Your answers

  • The General and Jupiter stay Destroyers at 1.5 tiles (0.21 and 0.25 off the rail on the 2×2 curve).
  • The Crocodile moves up to Battleship: 2.5 tiles on Ladder A (0.11 off the rail), 3 on Ladder B (0.15). It is still the steadiest Battleship by far.
  • No hinged halves for the Koutetsujou or the DDA40X.
  • The Koutetsujou's bogies spread evenly along its body, as you suggested, instead of all four sitting under the rear half. Its body then centres over the curve like any other engine's, so its overhang drops to the normal level for its length.
