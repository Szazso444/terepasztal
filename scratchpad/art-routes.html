Five routes to the Willowline look

Every route below can build a world. They differ in what produces the art, what draws it, and what each new locomotive costs you two years from now.

Target
The concept boards: painted 2.5D, warm key light, cream masonry, real occlusion.
Hard constraint
Fully agent-driven. Blender is a tool. No manual modelling, no cleanup by hand.
Planning assumption
Scope grows. Nuclear, maglev and hyperloop are content, not hypotheticals.
Fixed today
2,839 atlas frames, 64×32 tiles, 25 drawn facings, PixiJS only at runtime.

The routes

Push the pixel generators harder

Cannot reach it

Keep drawing every frame in TypeScript, double the tile to 128×64, add shades. This is the work just finished, scaled up. It makes the current look better and still lands short of the boards, because flat shading has no occlusion and no soft light to give.

On growth
Worst. Every new asset is drawn by hand, per frame

Asset programs, rendered headless, baked to atlases

Start here

An agent writes a parametric Python program per asset. Headless Blender runs it and renders the facings. The packer that already exists bakes them into the atlases the game already loads. Cycles gives real ambient occlusion and contact shadows offline, so the per frame ceiling is whatever the renderer can do with unlimited time. Proven in the repository, not argued — see below.

Same programs, drawn live in 3D

Keep open

The identical asset library, rendered at runtime by Three.js through an orthographic camera at the same angle. Lighting becomes a scene property, so dusk, headlights and a train's shadow on the viaduct beneath it are consequences rather than features. Depth is a buffer, which is the only clean answer to elevated track and a vehicle inside a tube.

Produces
The same programs, exported as meshes
Draws

Cost
Replaces ~9,000 lines of render layer
Rule
Adds a runtime dependency. Author-level decision
On growth
Best. A livery is a material swap, a facing is free

Buy the meshes from a generator

Supplement only

Image-to-3D services produce a usable mesh in seconds. They are good at organic shapes and poor at the mechanical ones this game is made of: wheels, rods, trusses, anything that has to tile exactly. The one controlled study puts first-pass time at 238 minutes against 716 for hand work, and states plainly that the cleanup debt it created was never costed.

Good for
Trees, rock, foliage, one-off scenery
Bad for
Locomotives, track, bridges, tiling pieces
Licensing
Free tiers are attribution-only or non-commercial
Consistency
Per-asset generators. A coherent set is not what they sell

Commission an artist

Breaks the constraint

The highest ceiling available and the fastest way to a look you can trust. It is also the one route that fails the brief, because it puts a person back in the loop for every asset and makes growth a permanent line item. It survives in one narrow form: paying once for two or three reference assets that define the target, which the loop then conforms to.

The loop also found its own limit. A quality check that judged colours read a shadowed limestone wall as a slate roof. Gates must read the renderer's material pass, never interpret pixels. Style cannot be judged by a critic at all, so it is enforced in shared code instead: one palette, one light rig, one camera, one material set.

The call

Two of these routes need the same thing, and that is what makes the decision safe. A rendered sprite and a live 3D scene both come from one library of asset programs. Build the library and the choice of what draws it stays open until the evidence is in.

  1. Build the library and bake it. Programs, headless render, existing packer, five small engine changes. Start with rolling stock: 25 drawn facings per vehicle is where a 3D source pays for itself and hand-drawing never will.
  2. Let generated meshes cover foliage only. Trees and rock, where a soft mesh is fine and a licence is cheap. Never the mechanical assets.
  3. Decide on runtime 3D when the library exists, against a real viaduct and a real second livery. That is a question with an answer by then, not a bet now.
  4. Keep one human checkpoint. Someone blesses two or three references and reviews a contact sheet of the whole set once a milestone. Coherence is a property of the set, and a per-asset critic never sees the set.
