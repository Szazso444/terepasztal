PLACEMENT MECHANICS · OPTIONS FOR DISCUSSION

Let the occupied tiles define the building

These are schematic examples, not final art or implemented game rules. Every occupied cell snaps to the same grid. The sprite’s ground contact must fit those cells; roof height may overlap tiles visually. The approved shoreline blend remains separate from placement.

A · One reserved lot

A 3 × 2 property is placed as one object. Buildings and yard reserve all six tiles. Platforms sit outside the selected frontage.

  • Simple, predictable occupancy and demolition.
  • Yard space cannot later be used for another building or track.
  • Larger buildings require larger whole-tile lots.

B · Building + optional yard

A 3 × 1 building reserves three tiles. An optional 3 × 1 forecourt is a separate ground treatment. Platforms still connect to an explicit frontage.

  • Building scale remains consistent without forcing a large property.
  • Decorative paving can be replaced by track or another building.
  • Keep visual blending within the neighbour’s placement rules.

C · Modular station

A 2 × 1 station building and three platform modules form a connected facility. The player adds modules on grid cells.

  • Capacity and usable length follow the actual platform modules.
  • Station buildings, loading yards and platforms can grow independently.
  • More flexible construction, with more connection rules to implement.

Common rule: one footprint definition must drive the placement ghost, collision checks, selection, demolition, sprite anchor and track connection points. Yards must not invent a second footprint.

Next decisions: reserved property or optional yard; automatic adjacent platforms or a chosen frontage; fixed footprints during upgrades or explicit expansion.
