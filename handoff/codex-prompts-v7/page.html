HOW THE GAME'S TREES ARE MADE. Match this family:
- Each tree is one picture from the image tool: one whole tree, centred, on a transparent
  background, with no ground, grass, shadow or water under it (assets/source/base-v1/tree-*.png,
  assets/source/cozy-v3/tree-*-v2.png). The reeds, which grow in water, are painted the same way:
  assets/source/base-v1/props-reeds.png stands on nothing.
- tools/illustrated-sprites.mjs trims each picture (alpha 64 and below becomes 0), scales it into
  the props atlas (public/assets/props.png, 4 texels per world px), widens it by 1.2 (every frame
  but the icons: wider = round(width * 1.2), the anchor's x moving with it, the height kept) and
  rounds its edges with tools/pixel-art.mjs (radius 2). The game draws every tree 1.5 times
  larger (TREE_SCALE in src/render/assetScale.ts). So every width in this prompt is widened, as
  the game draws it, and the check widens the same way. A kind of tree has three variants, keys
  props/<kind>_0 to _2 (the palm and the dead tree have two).
- The game's tree pictures are pixel art: blocky clusters with stair-stepped edges. The packer
  rounds those steps (smoothPng), and README "Style consistency: both smooth" asks for new nature
  art to be painted smooth. So the new trees are painted smooth, and the references are taken for
  their clusters, masses, camera, light and colour, not for their stepped edges. The image prompt
  below tells the image tool so.
- At zoom 1 a tile is 64x32 px and a person is 9 px tall (PERSON_PX). The trees there, as drawn,
  from the anchor to the top of the crown: round tree 32 to 42 px, oak 39 to 60, birch 48 to 67,
  pine 40 to 50, spruce 35 to 41. Across, widened: round tree 43 to 54 px, oak 61 to 68.
- A tree stands on its anchor, the trunk foot, which the game sets on the point where the tree
  stands: in the middle of the trunk, 4 world px (6 drawn px) above the trunk's lowest row for the
  round tree and the oak, 3 (4.5 drawn px) for the birch, pine and spruce (in
  public/assets/props.json, h - ay is 16 or 12 texels). So the trunk's lowest pixels show a little
  below that point.
- One light from the upper left. The family's greens, measured on tree-oak-v2.png and
  tree-round-v2.png: deep shade #22332E, mid #3E4630 and #565D31, light #7B7D34 and #7F913B, lit

THE CHECK. Write check-tree.mjs in the folder (Node, pngjs only, no new dependency). It imports
trimSource and resample from ../../../tools/illustrated-sprites.mjs and smoothPng from
../../../tools/pixel-art.mjs, finds the folder's files and the props atlas by its own location,
and reads the picture you name from where you run it, the repository root:
node assets/source/waterside-trees-2026-10-16/check-tree.mjs <file> --kind mangrove|willow
  --height <px> [--foot x,y] [--record]
1. Read the picture. Print "returned": [w, h]; "edgePixels", the pixels with alpha above 64 within
   8 px of any edge; and "partialAlphaPct", the share of pixels with alpha 1 to 250.
2. The box: the extent of the pixels with alpha above 64 (left, top, right, bottom), as
   trimSource cuts it.
3. The foot, unless --foot is given. Willow: go up from the box's bottom to the first row that
   has a pixel with alpha above 128; the foot is the middle of the longest run of such pixels in
   that row. Mangrove: in the lowest 25% of the box (the side tips of the root ellipse stand a
   quarter of its width above the bottom), take the leftmost x0 and the rightmost x1 of the
   pixels with alpha above 128; the foot is ((x0 + x1) / 2, bottom - (x1 - x0) / 4). Print the
   estimate as "footAuto" and the foot used as "foot".
4. Scale and anchor. Willow: k = (height + 6) / (foot.y - top), drawn px per picture px, and the
   anchor is (foot.x, foot.y - 6 / k), 6 drawn px above the foot. Mangrove: k = height /
   (foot.y - top), and the anchor is the foot. Print "anchor", and "drawn": width
   (right - left + 1) * k * 1.2; footFromCentre (anchor.x - (left + right) / 2) * k * 1.2;
   belowFoot (bottom - foot.y) * k; for a mangrove rootSpread (x1 - x0) * k * 1.2. The 1.2 is
   the packer's widening.
5. Colours, over the pixels with alpha above 128. "overCursorWhite": those with R >= 236,
   G >= 228 and B >= 208 (the cursor white #ECE4D0). "amberStrength": those with a hue of 25 to
   50 degrees, saturation at least 0.62 and value at least 0.85 (as strong as the selection amber
   #E8AA48). "cyanWaterPct": the percentage with a hue of 165 to 215 degrees, saturation at
   least 0.25 and value at least 0.35 (painted water, or the valid-placement cyan #66CCD6).
   "lumP99": the 99th percentile of 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B. Saturation is
   (max - min) / max, value is max / 255.
6. The look, checks/<name>-look.png. Trim the picture with trimSource; resample it to the atlas,
   width round(trimmedWidth * k * 4 / 1.5 * 1.2) and height round(trimmedHeight * k * 4 / 1.5);
   and round it with smoothPng(png, 2), as the packer does. Draw a scene 220 x 120 px on grass
   (124, 143, 60) with 64x32 tile diamonds. For a mangrove: a water tile (71, 123, 130) centred
   on (90, 90), a bank tile (98, 116, 48) up and to the left of it, and another water tile down
   and to the right. For a willow: a bank tile at (90, 90), a water tile down and to the right
   and a bank tile down and to the left. Stand the tree on (90, 90) by its anchor (its x widened
   with the picture), resampled by 1.5 / 4, so it is at its drawn size. Beside it stand a person,
   a 3 x 9 px slate block (98, 107, 108) with its foot at (30, 90), and the game's large oak,
   props/oak_2 cut from public/assets/props.png by its frame in public/assets/props.json, on its
   anchor at (170, 90), resampled by 1.5 / 4. Write that scene magnified 4 times (nearest
   neighbour), and beside it the same scene built at twice the size (zoom 2) magnified 2 times.
7. With --record, write the result into trees.json in the folder, under the file's name. The
   packing will take each tree's anchor from there.
