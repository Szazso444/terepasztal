WHY THIS WAY. The game's camera is 30 degrees down, 2:1. A picture painted from another angle is
repainted, never stretched, squeezed or sheared to fit: that is the author's standing rule (issue
#536), because the fit stretches whatever it is given and a stretched building looks wrong. The
derrick is the game's oil works and is seen from one side only today. Three things were learned on
the coaling stage and the Fuel Pump and are built into this prompt: paint on the guide, never on an
earlier attempt of yours; the painter spreads a building 15 to 26 per cent wider than its plinth
unless it is told the width and checked (so the width is measured first); and one correction at a
time, replacing the last, never a growing pile.

lane left it there, use it and fix only a wrong number). It reads a picture with pictureOf

within 12 (RGB distance) of #E8AA48; and PASS or FAIL for each test below that a machine can read.
Where measureBase puts a corner on a crane, a pipe, a canopy post or inside a quoin step, read the
outer corner of the wall's foot by eye on a zoomed crop and record both values.
