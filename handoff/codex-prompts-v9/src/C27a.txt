1. THE BOUNDARY TEST CHANGES (Core's recalibration of 21 October 2026; it replaces every boundary test and every "boundary deviation at most 15 canvas px" below). The old test took the single largest distance between the two outlines (the symmetric Hausdorff distance), so one thin protrusion (a chimney pot, a post, a hose, a bargeboard tip) refused pictures that matched the scaffold everywhere else. Measure as before (the same best uniform fit, the same 2 canvas px opening of the picture's mask), then take, for the picture's outline against the scaffold's and for the scaffold's against the picture's, the whole set of point distances in canvas px. PASS needs: the IoU at the best fit at least 0.90 (unchanged) AND the 95th percentile at most 15 canvas px (3 game px; 5 canvas px make a game px) for each of the two sets (the larger of the two is the figure) AND the largest distance of both sets at most 30 canvas px (6 game px). Record IoU at s0, f, dx, dy, the IoU at the best fit, the maximum, and the two 95th percentiles. The percentile is the value at rank ceil(0.95 n) of the sorted distances. Every other test, and every identity detail the prompt names (a door, a window row, the high gable window), stays as written: a picture that passes the outline test and misses a named detail is still refused. The measuring script of this group's earlier prompts, if it is in your tree, can be copied and changed for this: change only the last step.

THE MEASURING SCRIPT. Write assets/source/rotations-535/power_plant/measure-power_plant.mjs, your own. The shared assets/source/rotations-535/measure.mjs belongs to no job: other windows work on this branch at the same time and rewrote its numbers under each other, so do not use, edit or commit it. Your script reads a returned picture's alpha with pictureOf (tools/building-check.mjs; opaque means alpha above 128) and the view's scaffold json from ../scaffolds-535, and prints, per picture: the returned size and s0 = returned width / 1536; the silhouette numbers below; the opaque box; the base's width and k (THE SIZE); the two feet's slopes (rise over run; 0.50 for a 30 degree camera), reported and never refused; the height number below; the counts of opaque pixels with red, green and blue all at or above #ECE4D0 and within 12 (RGB distance) of #E8AA48; and PASS or FAIL for each machine test below. There is NO separate camera test, foot-ratio test or footprint test any more: the scaffold carries the camera and the plan, and the silhouette decides.

SILHOUETTE, the first test. The scaffold's silhouette is the polygon outline_polygon_px of the view's json (canvas px, 1536 x 1024), filled and scaled by s0 to the returned picture. The picture's silhouette is its opaque pixels with the enclosed holes filled (the polygon has none). Compute: (a) the overlap IoU (intersection over union of the two filled shapes) at s0 with no shift, reported; (b) the best fit: one uniform factor f between 0.85 and 1.15 on both axes and a shift (dx, dy), which is all Art's packing does to a picture (a uniform scale, then the base placed on the footprint), chosen to maximise the IoU (a coarse-to-fine search is fine); record f, dx, dy and the IoU at the best fit; (c) the boundary deviation at the best fit: the boundary distances, in canvas px (divide pixels of the returned picture by s0 x f): for every point of the picture's outline its distance to the scaffold's outline, and for every point of the scaffold's outline its distance to the picture's outline: two sets of distances; record the maximum of both (the old symmetric Hausdorff number) and the 95th percentile of each set (sort the distances, take the value at rank ceil(0.95 n)); the boundary figure is the larger of the two 95th percentiles (CORE REVIEW 1, rule 1), after an opening of 2 canvas px on the picture's mask so that antialiasing specks do not count. PASS needs the IoU at the best fit of at least 0.90 AND a boundary deviation with the 95th percentile at most 15 canvas px AND the maximum at most 30 canvas px (3 game px; 5 canvas px make a game px on this canvas). A picture that fails is repainted, never nudged, stretched or reshaped to fit: the shape comes from the painting over the scaffold.
