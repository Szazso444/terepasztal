THE TOOL FOR THE NUMBERS. Write v3/measure-v3.mjs. Copy people-2026-10-11/pose-png.mjs and contact-overlap.mjs into v3/ (their paths then stay in v3/); take isOverWhite and isAmberStrong from tools/atlas-cap.mjs, and the guide joints and groundPoint from assets/source/people-v3-guides/manifest.json (guides entry back-shoulder-p<n>.png). Measure on the picture as returned; if it is another 2:3 size, scale columns and rows by width / 1024 first; any other shape fails. "Opaque" is alpha above 128. Tests per picture (guide numbers in brackets, P1, P2, P3, P4):
1. Height: box() height of the picture within 3% of the same on the guide (measure each back-shoulder guide with your tool; the axe may stand above the head, so measure the figure without anything above the cap's top).
2. Soles: the lowest opaque row within 8 px of row 1440, the guide's (a quarter of a texel at the game's zoom 4).
3. Planted foot. For a foot (right at P1, P2, P3; left at P3, P4) take the opaque pixels inside the window x from the least to the most of that foot's heel, toe and ankle canvas x in the manifest, minus and plus 30; y from 145 above the lowest of those joints' y, to 30 below it. Let ylow be the lowest opaque row in the window; the foot's point (cx, cy) is the mean of the opaque pixels of the window in rows ylow - 40 to ylow; f = ((cx - 512) - 2 * (cy - gy)) / 1717.6, gy the guide's groundPoint row. Slide of a pair of poses (1,2), (2,3), (3,4) is f of the earlier minus f of the later, on the same foot. The back-swing guides give f 0.1106, -0.0068, -0.0403 (right, P1 to P3) and 0.0769, 0.0148 (left, P3, P4), slides 0.1173, 0.0335, 0.0621; the back-shoulder guides have the same legs, so your tool should give the same on them; use the values it gives on the back-shoulder guides. The gait's step is 0.123 of the height per pose; the test is that each slide of the picture is within 0.01 of the guide's. A pair is checked when its later pose is painted, against the kept earlier picture; a miss belongs to the later pose.
4. Repeat: contactOverlap() of the kept P1 with P3 below 0.45 (hatTop and soleRow taken from each picture's box); the back-swing guides give 0.221. Report the P2 with P4 figure too (the guide's is 0.504); it is not a pass rule.
5. Turned: the topmost opaque row in columns 352 to 412 (L) and in columns 614 to 668 (R); (R - L) / 960 within 0.03 of the same measure on the back-shoulder guide of the pose (the back-swing guides give 0.070; a figure straight away from the camera gives under 0); on a painted figure the axe's head and the cap lie in those columns, so their top row is not the shoulder: report the machine reading, but make the test by eye. On a zoomed crop of the picture beside its guide read the row of the top of the picture-left shoulder (L) and of the picture-right shoulder (R), where the shoulder's line meets the neck and the sleeve, under the axe's head and the cap; record both numbers and the same two read on the back-shoulder guide; the test is (R - L) / 960 within 0.03 of the guide's reading. A reading you cannot make because the axe covers both shoulders is not a stop: say so, and judge the turn by eye (the back of the head and the right side, no face from the front). By eye also: the back of the head and the right side, no face from the front.
6. Caps (a REPORT, not a pass rule, and never a reason to stop): record the number of pixels with alpha 16 or more for which isOverWhite is true, the number for which isAmberStrong is true, and the number of pixels with alpha 1 to 15 (haze). tools/atlas-cap.mjs (issue #465) scales both colours down and zeroes alpha below 16 at packing, which is why the earlier runs' 3 to 49 near-white and up to 1616 amber pixels are not a reason to stop. If either count is over 300, add one plain sentence to the next Correction ("your shirt's orange came out as strong as the selection amber"), and go on.
7. Real RGBA: PNG colour type 6, all four corners alpha 0.
