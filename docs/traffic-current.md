# Train traffic control: current behaviour

How the simulation decides that a train must slow or stop for another train and how it resolves contention at junctions, as implemented in `src/sim/traffic.ts`, `src/sim/fleet.ts` and `src/sim/trains.ts`. Descriptive only; no changes are proposed.

## 1. Overview

`Fleet.tick` (`src/sim/fleet.ts:576`) runs in this order: rebuild the tile occupancy map from car poses (`src/sim/fleet.ts:578`, `src/sim/fleet.ts:278-289`); rebuild per-tile "reservations" for the next 8 tiles of every moving train's path, first come first served (`src/sim/fleet.ts:579`, `src/sim/fleet.ts:322-329`); `Traffic.assign` first lets every train loaded from a save take up its platform, reserve again the escape it was backing off along, and plan its path again to the fuel or water service or the stop it was bound for (`Train.resumeAfterLoad`, once per train, so a loaded train's path is claimed from its first tick, a train backing off runs on along its escape for the same group, and a train waiting for a platform sees the one loading there), then recomputes claims and every train's `claimLimit`/`claimBlocker` (`src/sim/fleet.ts:580`, `src/sim/traffic.ts:166`); every train ticks (`src/sim/fleet.ts:582-585`); `Traffic.observe` does blocked/stuck bookkeeping (`src/sim/fleet.ts:586`); proactive parking of trains held on their blocker's path (`src/sim/fleet.ts:587-602`); jam-chain resolution (`src/sim/fleet.ts:603-670`). Claims are computed from the previous tick's positions and paths and consumed by `tickMove` in the same tick.

## 2. Track sections

`rebuildSections` (`src/sim/traffic.ts:81-126`) runs whenever `track.version` changes, which also clears every claim (`src/sim/traffic.ts:167-170`). A tile is a node when it has no track, has more than one link (switches and crossings both have two, `src/world/track.ts:20-36`), is a station gate tile with track ("platform", `src/sim/traffic.ts:85-87`) or has fewer than two connected edges (dead end) (`src/sim/traffic.ts:88-95`). Nodes get the singleton id `-(key+1)` (`src/sim/traffic.ts:101`); plain tiles are flood-filled along `track.connected` edges into numbered sections (`src/sim/traffic.ts:104-125`), kept in `sections` (tile→id) and `sectionTiles` (id→tiles) (`src/sim/traffic.ts:61-62`). `sectionOf` falls back to the singleton id (`src/sim/traffic.ts:132-134`); `sectionLength` (`src/sim/traffic.ts:136-139`) has no callers. `HORIZON = 6` tiles (`src/sim/traffic.ts:7`) bounds claim extent (§3).

## 3. Claims

`claims` maps tile key → train id (`src/sim/traffic.ts:60`).

**Release.** Each tick a keep-set per train is built from its car tiles (poses rounded with `Math.floor(p+0.5)`, `src/sim/traffic.ts:143-150`) plus, only while `state === 'moving'`, its whole `pathAhead()` (`src/sim/traffic.ts:174-179`); every claim not in its owner's keep-set, or owned by a removed train, is deleted (`src/sim/traffic.ts:180-183`). Hence a train that stops moving (arrival sets `loading`/`waiting`, `src/sim/trains.ts:1267-1270`; also `idle`, `yielding`, `noFuel`) keeps only the tiles under its cars; a retreating train keeps only tiles that were already claimed and lie on its new hold path. Platform slots are a per-level count (`src/sim/stations.ts:183-185`, `src/sim/stations.ts:243-245`), independent of tile claims. An idle train holds no slot (§9).

**Order.** Trains are processed moving-first, then ascending `yieldCount`, then descending weight, then ascending id (`src/sim/traffic.ts:184-190`). Car tiles are always claimed, overwriting other holders; if the holder's cars are also there an overlap episode is logged (`src/sim/traffic.ts:192-200`).

**Extent.** `claimLimit`/`claimBlocker` reset to `Infinity`/`null` (`src/sim/traffic.ts:201-202`); trains not moving or `holding` claim nothing ahead (`src/sim/traffic.ts:203`). Otherwise the walk covers the full remaining path (`pathAhead()`, `src/sim/trains.ts:855-868`, one entry per tile with its arc). At a section change it stops when the tile is more than `HORIZON` of arc ahead and the previous tile was a node (`sectionStart < 0`) (`src/sim/traffic.ts:214-219`). Since a plain section always ends in a node, the result is: at least 6 tiles of arc, then to the end of the plain section being traversed plus its bounding node tile.

**Contention while walking** (`src/sim/traffic.ts:220-249`). A tile held by another train that is moving with heading dot product > 0.1 (`headingOf`, `src/sim/traffic.ts:152-159`) is same-direction following: `claimLimit` = distance to that tile, nothing rolled back, break (`src/sim/traffic.ts:228-233`). Any other holder (oncoming or standing) triggers the whole-section rule: if the tile is plain, tiles of that section were added, and the train's rounded head tile (`Math.floor(x+0.5)`) is not itself in that section (`headIn`, `src/sim/traffic.ts:235-237`), all tiles added for the section are unclaimed except those under the train's own cars, and `claimLimit` becomes the arc of the section's entry tile (`src/sim/traffic.ts:238-244`). If the blocked tile is a node, or the head is already inside the section, the claim stops just short of the blocked tile (`src/sim/traffic.ts:245`). `counters.waits` increments here once per train per tick (`src/sim/traffic.ts:247`).

## 4. Slowing and stopping

`tickMove` (`src/sim/trains.ts:1050`) computes `blockDist`. It scans path points from `pathPos+0.3` to `pathPos+LOOKAHEAD` (2.5 tiles, `src/sim/trains.ts:123`, `src/sim/trains.ts:1056-1057`), skipping its own car tiles (`src/sim/trains.ts:1060-1068`), and stops at the first tile in `ctx.occupied`: `blockDist = arc − pathPos − HOLD_GAP` (0.45, `src/sim/trains.ts:125`, `src/sim/trains.ts:1069-1072`). Unless `holding`, the claim limit is applied the same way when smaller: `blockDist = claimLimit − HOLD_GAP`, `blocker = claimBlocker` (`src/sim/trains.ts:1077-1080`). `blocked` is true only when `blockDist < 0.3` (`src/sim/trains.ts:1081-1082`).

**Speed.** `vmax` combines engine speed, load, path factor (curves, bridges, hills), weather, biome, eco mode and reversing (`src/sim/trains.ts:1148-1155`). `vStop = sqrt(2·DECEL·min(remaining, blockDist))` (`src/sim/trains.ts:1156`); the target is the smaller, approached at `ACCEL = 0.7` or `DECEL·1.5` (`src/sim/trains.ts:119-120`, `src/sim/trains.ts:1157-1159`). So there is a braking curve toward the hold point, and the step is additionally hard-clamped to `blockDist` (`src/sim/trains.ts:1160-1165`), so no overrun is possible; a floor of `0.02·gdt` keeps trains creeping whenever `blockDist > 0`. A head that comes within `ARRIVE` (0.001 tiles) of the end of its path runs the rest in the same step, so a train that arrives stands with its head on the end of its path, the last tile's centre. Occupancy is seen only 2.5 tiles ahead; a claim limit is seen from any distance.

**While blocked** `blockedTime` accumulates; after `REROUTE_AFTER = 6 s` one reroute avoiding occupied (not claimed) tiles is tried, repeated every `RETRY_EVERY = 20 s` (`src/sim/trains.ts:127-129`, `src/sim/trains.ts:1119-1144`). Dynamic trains additionally probe every 4 s for an oncoming train that reserved the next 8 tiles and, if found, try a forward-only detour avoiding its reserved tiles (`src/sim/trains.ts:1083-1118`).

**Other stops.** Fuel or power failure sets `noFuel`/`noPower`, speed 0 (`src/sim/trains.ts:497-508`, `src/sim/trains.ts:1168-1174`), retried every 3 s (`src/sim/trains.ts:965-976`); a tank under 30 % gives eco mode at 0.6 speed (`src/sim/trains.ts:131-133`, `src/sim/trains.ts:1145`). At stations `waiting` lasts until a platform slot frees (`src/sim/trains.ts:919-925`); dwell is bounded by `MIN_DWELL 2`, `MAX_DWELL 40`, `MAX_DWELL_FULL 240` and the `waitFull` rule (`src/sim/trains.ts:121`, `src/sim/trains.ts:134-136`, `src/sim/trains.ts:1442-1450`); `idle` asks again where to go every 10 s (§9).

## 5. Junction contention

Switches and crossings carry no state; the path chooses the exit (`src/world/pathfinding.ts:45`, `src/world/track.ts:101-110`). A junction is one node tile, so two paths across it are serialised by the tile claim: the first claimant in the §3 sort gets it and the other stops `HOLD_GAP` short (or at its section entry if a section lies between). No other arbitration of junction priority exists.

Contention is untangled by `Fleet.tick`:

1. **Proactive parking** (`src/sim/fleet.ts:587-602`): a moving, non-holding train with `claimLimit ≤ 1.5` whose cars stand on its blocker's `pathTileKeys` (cars + remaining path + wanted path, `src/sim/trains.ts:1612-1624`) is marked blocked and calls `retreat`, provided `yieldUntil` has passed; success logs a yield.
2. **Chain resolution** (`src/sim/fleet.ts:603-670`): trains blocked for `MUTUAL_GRACE = 4 s` (`src/sim/fleet.ts:679`) start a chain along `blockedBy` (≤ 8 links, loops detected, `src/sim/fleet.ts:612-625`). An `idle`/`waiting` tail with someone behind it moves first: `retreat`, else `parkElsewhere` (`src/sim/fleet.ts:629-642`). Otherwise the chain is dead when it loops, its tail is itself blocked past grace, or its tail is in a non-moving, non-transient state (`src/sim/fleet.ts:645-650`); the chain sorted by ascending `yieldCount`, ascending weight, descending id (`src/sim/fleet.ts:654-656`) is asked to `retreat` in turn and the first success wins, one per chain per tick (`src/sim/fleet.ts:657-663`). If nobody could and the chain loops, `Traffic.deadlock` is reported in the single tick where the head's `blockedTime` crosses 16 s (`src/sim/fleet.ts:664-669`), deduplicated per chain signature for 60 s (`src/sim/traffic.ts:316-337`).

`retreat` (`src/sim/trains.ts:1529-1610`) returns false immediately when no car is on the other train's path ("already clear", `src/sim/trains.ts:1553-1554`). A hold tile must be off their path, unoccupied, not claimed by anyone else (`ctx.claimedBy`) and not a switch/crossing (`src/sim/trains.ts:1545-1551`). It paths forward to the nearest hold tile (cost ≤ 600), else backwards with a consist flip (`src/sim/trains.ts:1556-1565`), then tries to go deeper, `ceil(ownLen)` tiles down to 1 (`src/sim/trains.ts:1569-1582`), and fails if the rear cars would still stand on their path (`src/sim/trains.ts:1584-1586`). Success sets `holding`, `yieldCount++`, `yieldUntil = now+30`, state `moving` (`src/sim/trains.ts:1600-1609`). At the end of the hold path the train enters `yielding` (`src/sim/trains.ts:1216-1219`) and every 2 s tries to dispatch avoiding occupied tiles, or any path once 60 s past `yieldUntil` (`src/sim/trains.ts:929-950`); meanwhile `noteBlockerOnPlainPath` keeps `blockedBy` on the first occupant of its wanted path so chain resolution still sees it (`src/sim/trains.ts:997-1023`). A holding train ignores `claimLimit` (`src/sim/trains.ts:1077`) and claims nothing ahead (`src/sim/traffic.ts:203`): it moves on occupancy alone.

A refuge must also leave the train a way on (`Train.onwardTest`, passed to `findRefugePath` as `accept`): from where the move would leave it, the train can run, either end first, to its next stop (its fuel detour, if it has one), or, when idle, back to the station it stood at (its next stop if it stood at none). Rear first counts from the tile the rear would stand on, so a stop whose platform would lie under the cars, which the rear runs away from, does not count. The rear's place is measured along the trail the way lays, on which the cars stand: eight chords a tile through the tile's geometry points (`trailLength`), not the arcs that pathfinding costs. A narrow curve's chords come to 0.7841 a tile against an arc of π/4 = 0.7854, enough on a winding narrow line to carry the rear end over a tile edge. The trail a moving train lays runs along the same chords: besides the head's place each step, it lays each geometry point the head passes at which the path turns or crosses a tile edge (`layTrail`), and a head arrives on the centre the count starts from (§4), so for a train that ran to where it stands the count from the tiles it ran puts the rear end where it stands, to rounding. The tile a train sets off from when it reverses (`reversedTrail`) is the one its rear end lies on, read from where the rear end lies (`crossingBehind`), not from a point of the trail near it. Turning round cuts the trail at the rear end, so where the rear end stands on the trail's start the tile beyond is read on along the trail's first chord: a train that turns round twice where it stands sets off from the same tile as before. A rear end on a tile edge, or short of one by no more than `EDGE_SLACK` (10⁻⁶ tiles), stands on the tile beyond it, so a stop whose edge the last car ends on does not count either: a narrow-gauge train a whole number of tiles and a half long (five wagons, an odd number of them mine tubs) stops with its rear on an edge. A head on an edge stands on the tile it is leaving (`samplePath`). `statesReaching` (`src/sim/recovery.ts`) finds once per tick every directed track state from which that stop is reached without reversing, so each refuge the search offers is a lookup; one turned down does not end the search, which goes on to the next. The check is skipped when the train has no way to that stop from where it stands either. A train is therefore never backed into a siding whose switch faces away from its stop.

When no train of a blocking group can pull aside and one of them has been held 16 s, `Traffic.deadlock` counts it (once a minute per group), and every train held in it, moving or yielding, shows `STR.traffic.jammed` ("Jammed: no siding it can pull into to let the other train by") until it moves half a tile, does something else or leaves the jam, when it gets its own note back (`Fleet.keepJamNotes`); an idle train in the jam keeps its own note (§9). A working train that pulls aside in a jam for the train it last pulled aside for, before it has reached a stop (`Train.gaveWay`; reaching a stop clears what it last pulled aside for, and making way while idle does not count), is kept back: the line keeps the two from passing. It still pulls aside, so the other runs on, but `Traffic.deadlock` counts the jam as it sets off, and where its way ends it says so (`STR.traffic.keptBack`, "Kept back: it gives way to the same train each time, with nowhere free to pass it"); a save keeps that for a train on its way back (`kept` in the escape's save). A `yielding` train with no track at all on to its stop says so (`STR.traffic.noWayOn`), and a train setting off from a yield drops the notes about it (pulling aside, kept back, waiting for a new plan, no way on, jammed).

`parkElsewhere` (`src/sim/fleet.ts`) would send an idle/waiting tail to the nearest other station with a free slot and a platform tile that is off the waiting train's path and unoccupied; nothing calls it any more.

## 6. Stuck detection and statistics

`observe` (`src/sim/traffic.ts:339-387`): `blocked` adds `gdt` to blocked seconds (`src/sim/traffic.ts:346`); head movement over `PROGRESS_EPS = 0.05` resets the progress clock (`src/sim/traffic.ts:349-352`); a train that wants to move (`moving`/`yielding`/`waiting`, `src/sim/traffic.ts:348`) with no movement for `STUCK_AFTER = 30 s` (`src/sim/traffic.ts:9`) opens a stuck episode (`src/sim/traffic.ts:369-384`), closed by a move over 0.5 tiles (`src/sim/traffic.ts:353-365`) or by ceasing to want to move (`src/sim/traffic.ts:366-368`). Overlaps are deduplicated per pair for 5 s (`src/sim/traffic.ts:281-287`). Episodes are capped at 400 (`src/sim/traffic.ts:275-280`); `verbose` prints them (`src/sim/traffic.ts:58`, `src/sim/traffic.ts:278`); `onEpisode` is a hook nothing sets. `stuckTrains()` (`src/sim/traffic.ts:389-394`) feeds `Notices.refresh` every 0.5 s (`src/game.ts:1881-1889`), which raises a `bad` stuck notice or an `info` held notice after 8 s blocked (`src/sim/notices.ts:62-70`); the advisor tips past 20 s (`src/game.ts:1662-1663`). The debug panel row shows only the counters (`src/game.ts:2285-2289`); `report()` (`src/sim/traffic.ts:396-416`, last 60 episodes) is console-only. `src/sim/stats.ts` holds resource flows, no traffic data.

## 7. Interaction with dynamic routing

`badTargets` (`src/sim/trains.ts:383-387`) expire after 60 s when a station gave nothing, or a freight train put goods down there, and would be picked again (`src/sim/trains.ts:1469-1473`), and after 240 s when no path exists at departure (`src/sim/trains.ts:1515-1521`) or after 20 s of yielding with no blocker (`src/sim/trains.ts:955-961`). `dynamicTargets` collects the targets of other dynamic trains in `moving`/`yielding`/`waiting` (`src/sim/fleet.ts:331-342`); `chooseNext` skips those stations for pick-ups (`src/sim/fleet.ts:449`, `src/sim/fleet.ts:474`) but not for drops or contract destinations (`src/sim/fleet.ts:414-434`, `src/sim/fleet.ts:470`). A freight train (the one mode that hauls goods between producers, warehouses and depots) that put goods down at a stop takes nothing on there, does not pick that stop on leaving it, and keeps that in its save (`dropped`) until it sets off, so what it put into a warehouse is not hauled straight back out. Neither `chooseNext` nor `dispatch` consults claims; `avoid` predicates exclude only occupied tiles (`src/sim/trains.ts:1507-1512`, `src/sim/trains.ts:934`, `src/sim/trains.ts:1124`) or, for the anticipate detour, reserved tiles (`src/sim/trains.ts:1103-1104`). Pathfinding is Dijkstra over (tile, entry edge) with a 0.2 penalty on a switch's curved leg (`src/world/pathfinding.ts:17-61`).

## 8. Known limits

- No lineside signals or aspects; a train learns of conflicts only through its own path walk in `assign`.
- No headway or block occupancy beyond the whole-section rule; same-direction spacing relies on the 2.5-tile occupancy scan (`src/sim/trains.ts:123`).
- Claims are recomputed every tick from a priority sort; a section can be rolled back the tick after it was granted (`src/sim/traffic.ts:238-244`).
- Junction priority is not configurable: it is the sort at `src/sim/traffic.ts:184-190`; chain resolution uses the opposite weight order (`src/sim/fleet.ts:654-656`).
- Routing never avoids claimed track, only occupied tiles, so reroutes can lead straight into a held section.
- Braking is a per-tick curve with a hard clamp, not a signalled braking distance; occupancy lookahead ignores speed.
- `counters.waits` (debug "holds") counts ticks, not events (`src/sim/traffic.ts:247`); `headOn` is declared (`src/sim/traffic.ts:13`, `src/sim/traffic.ts:66`) but never raised.
- No train reverses partway along a move: a train turns only before it sets off (the consist flip of `dispatch` and of the refuge search, §5). So a dead-end siding takes a train only when it can run straight in, and two trains meeting head-on on single track pass each other only at a loop: the one that turns into a siding can leave it only the way it came.
- A jam that only a reversal partway would clear (on single track, a train whose only siding faces away from it, or two working trains meeting head-on with a dead-end siding and no loop) is not resolved. It is counted as a deadlock and the held trains say they are jammed (§5). A train that pulled aside sets off again 60 s past its yield whatever stands in its way, so such a jam can repeat: it meets the same train, pulls aside again, and the deadlock is counted again.
- A save keeps what each train is doing, the way it runs and what it waits for, but not the timers and memory kept besides, which start afresh after a load: the trains' (when a roaming train under way next looks for a train coming at it, every 4 s; how long a yielding one has found no way on, a roaming one idling after 20 s; whether a held train has tried a way round yet; what a roaming train has taken on at its stop, though that a freight train put goods down there is kept (`dropped`); which train it last pulled aside for in a jam, so a loaded train gives way to that train once more before it is kept back), the fleet's (when each station was last served, which a roaming train's pick favours; which trains are jammed, whose note is blank until the jam resolution says so again) and the traffic control's (when each train last moved, the order claims are granted in; the tiles behind its head an escape still holds under the cars, as an escape is reserved again only from the head on; how long an escape has gone without progress). Where one of these decides, a loaded game can part from the one saved.

## 9. Idle trains

A roaming train idles (state `idle`, "Idle: nothing worth hauling yet") when its route mode picks nothing (`Fleet.chooseNext` returns null) or it has no way to its pick. `Train.idle` keeps it where it stands and releases what it does not need: its slot at the station it stands at, so a train arriving there can load, its path and any wanted path. Every 10 s it asks again (`Train.depart`); when it picks the station it stands at (cargo has turned up there) it loads there at once if a slot is free, else waits for one.

After the trains move, `Fleet.makeWay` (every 0.25 s of game time, on the ticks that pass a multiple of it) finds each idle train standing on a tile that another train's route crosses: its path, or the way it waits to take from a siding (`yielding`) or out of the way (another idle train). That train plans a move aside (`Train.planAside`, the refuge search of §5 from either end, with no reversal partway along, §8): the nearest place that holds the whole consist off every other train's route and leaves it a way back to the station it stood at (§5), in a siding first (plain dead-end track hanging off a switch with no platform on it, `Fleet.sidingTiles`), else on plain track, a platform or a depot gate of a station no other train is bound for. The way there crosses no other train, escape or claim of a train outside the group it makes way for; that group's claims give way. It is reserved and run as an escape (`Train.retreat`, note `STR.traffic.makingWay`), so the others hold back until it has passed, and at its end the train idles there (note `STR.traffic.madeWay`) instead of heading back to where it stood. A failed attempt is retried after 2 s.

When the trains it makes way for stand in its way out, it plans the way it would take were they gone, ending off their lines (not under them, nor on the plain line behind them up to the first switch, `Train.lineBehind`), and publishes it as part of its route (`Train.wantAside`, `pathTileKeys`; note `STR.traffic.waitAside`). The jam resolution then backs one of them off once it has waited 4 s, and the idle train moves aside after. With no way out at all it stays where it is (note `STR.traffic.noWayAside`): a layout with no siding, free platform or depot gate the train can run straight into still jams, a single line whose only siding faces away from the idle train among them, and the jam is counted with the held trains saying so (§5). The jam resolution also moves an idle train first, and ends that escape idle too. A train standing with no stop at all (`noRoute` with an empty schedule, as `Train.depart` leaves one) that the jam resolution backs off makes way in the same way: it says it is moving out of the way, idles where the escape ends, and stands with no route again when it next asks, 10 s on.

A save keeps that a train is making way (`aside` in the train's save, since format v17). Loaded on its way aside, it runs on along its escape, reserved again from the head on, saying it is making way, and idles where the escape ends; when the escape cannot be reserved again it stops and idles where it stands (note `STR.traffic.replan`). Loaded idle at a station, it stands there again holding no slot: `Train.resumeAfterLoad` gives a slot back only to a train that was loading. The save also keeps the way an idle train wants aside and the way a yielding train waits to take, with the train it last found in that way (`want`, `blockedBy`), so the jam resolution and `Fleet.makeWay` see them on the first tick after a load; the note each train shows (`note`), but that it is jammed; and the way every other train runs (`way`: the rest of its path and how far the head has left along it), so a train routed round one standing in its way stays on that route. A way that no longer ends where the train is bound, or that the track no longer carries under the head, is planned again as the shortest way to the service or stop it is bound for (`Train.replanAfterLoad`), as every load before v17 planned it. The looks of `Fleet.makeWay` fall on the same ticks with or without a load, as they follow the game clock, and each train keeps when it may try again (`asideRetry`, 2 s after its last try) and when a jam it is in may be searched again for a train to back off (`recoveryRetry`, 4 s after the last search, `Traffic.canRecover`), both saved since v17, so after a load an idle train in another train's way and the jam resolution try again when they would have.

## 10. Walkers at level crossings

Trains never wait for walkers; walkers wait for trains. A level crossing is a road across a plain straight of regular or narrow track (`Roads.crossingKeys`, `src/sim/roads.ts`). `CrossingWatch` (`src/sim/crossings.ts`) keeps which of them are closed. In play mode `SimStep.run` (`src/sim/step.ts`) moves it on once a step, before `people.tick` and so from where the trains stood at the end of the step before, and hands `people.tick` its `closed(x, y)`, and the watch itself for `detour` and `occupied` (below); the step reads the crossing keys again whenever the roads or the track change (their `version`s). Nothing of it is saved. A crossing is closed:

- while a car of any train stands on it (`Train.occupancyKeys`);
- while a train with its way set could reach it within `CROSSING_HORIZON` game seconds, running or standing on that way: its tile lies on the train's `pathAhead` no more than `crossingReach` plus `CROSSING_LEAD` (half a tile, `pathAhead` giving each tile's arc an eighth of a tile past its near edge) beyond the head. A train has its way set in the states `ROUTED_STATES` names: `moving`, which takes in a train held at a signal or behind another train and one just given a way out of a station or round traffic (`setPath` leaves it at a standstill), and `noFuel`, `noPower` and `overweight`, from which a train goes on along the way it kept. `crossingReach` is the run from the train's speed now, 0 for one standing, gaining at the hardest it could (`crossingPull`) up to its top speed downhill (`maxSpeed` times `DESCENT_SPEED`). `crossingPull` is the most `consistPhysics` gives the train with any of its units pulling, not only those pulling now, as a train stopped for fuel or power has none pulling while it waits, and with a steam unit aboard as if its tank wagons of water were empty, as its boiler tops up from them while it runs (`Train.drawTankerWater`) and the consist gets lighter. So a train setting off, or running below its top speed (backwards, loaded, in rain), closes a crossing further off than its speed alone would;
- and for `CROSSING_HOLD` (1.5 game seconds) from the first step that finds it clear of both.

A train with no way set closes only the tiles under its cars, whatever speed it was left with: loading or waiting at a platform, idle, yielding on a siding, with no route, or stranded. Walkers cross in front of a train standing at a station. A train held with its way set keeps the crossings within its reach closed for as long as it stands there, a train stopped for fuel or power among them; walkers walk round a crossing that only trains out of fuel or power keep closed (below).

`CROSSING_HORIZON` is `CROSSING_WALK` plus `CROSSING_MARGIN` (2 s), 9.07 s. `CROSSING_WALK` is the longest a walker is on a crossing tile: its longest way over the tile, corner to corner through the centre (√2 tiles, as a walk runs from tile centre to tile centre), at the slowest pace a walker has there, off a road (`WALK_SPEED`, 0.2 tiles a second): 7.07 s. A walker that set foot on an open crossing is therefore off it before any train that then had its way set can reach it.

A walker goes no further than the edge of a closed crossing it does not stand on yet, whichever tile of its walk that is (`clearAhead` in `src/sim/people.ts`): it stands there with its path, state and timer as they were, draws no random number, and goes on in the step the crossing opens, `CROSSING_HOLD` and one step after the last car left it. A walker already on a crossing goes on. Boarding and alighting figures wait the same way.

Walkers walk round a crossing that only trains standing out of fuel or power hold closed, as such a train may stand there for ever (#447). Those are the states `DETOUR_STATES` names: `noFuel`, out of fuel or water, and `noPower`, an electric engine out of power. `CrossingWatch.detour(x, y)` is true for a closed crossing every train closing it is in one of them, by a car on it or by its reach, and false for one in its hold or closed by any other train as well: a train passing, one held at a signal or behind another train, one stopped for its pull (`overweight`) or one standing in any state with no way set keeps walkers waiting as above. A walker that stops at the edge of such a crossing, the step it reaches it or the step the crossing becomes one while it waits there, looks at once for a walk from the tile it stands at to the end of its walk (`findWalk`, in the same box and keeping to roads as any walk does) that steps on no crossing closed at that moment and no tile a car stands on (`CrossingWatch.occupied`, read from the same trains), and takes it in place of its walk: straight on from the edge to that walk's next tile, or back to the middle of the tile it stands at first where the way straight on would cut into a tile it keeps off. The tile it stands at is the one it walked off onto the edge, which it never left: it steps back into it even where a car stands on it or it is a crossing it walked onto that dry trains alone hold since, and a crossing so held never holds it out of that tile. A crossing there that a train able to move holds, as above, keeps it on the edge until the crossing opens or dry trains alone hold it; meanwhile it walks round only straight on from the edge. It keeps to that walk when the crossing opens, and never sets foot on a closed crossing on it but the one it stands at, as on any walk. With no such walk, it waits on the edge as above and tries again every `DETOUR_RETRY` (5 game seconds, a walker's time over a tile off a road) while it waits there. A try draws no random number and changes nothing of the walker but its walk; nothing of it is saved. A walk round may cross bare track, as any walk may (`walkable` in `src/sim/people.ts`). Limit: a walker that steps back into a crossing dry trains alone hold is on it again for up to √2 tiles, no further than from the edge or corner it was held at to its middle and from there off it, at most half a diagonal each (7.07 s off a road, as long as `CROSSING_WALK`), and `CROSSING_HORIZON` does not cover that, as the crossing was closed when it stepped in: a train among them that gets its fuel or power back meanwhile sets off along its way from within its reach of the crossing.

Nobody is set down on a level crossing either, closed or open, and none of this draws a number of its own. A walker steps out of its home by the home's street door (`src/sim/doors.ts`, turned with the home) and goes back in by it. Stepping out to idle, it stands on the door's tile, short of its edge, when the tile outside the door is a level crossing: the draw that places it from 0.3 to 0.7 of a tile out from the centre of the door's tile, across the door's side, places it from 0.3 to 0.5 instead. A walk out of a place or back to it, a station's too, starts or ends on the tile outside its street door, or on the door's own tile when the tile outside is a level crossing, off the map or ground nobody walks on (`doorstep` in `src/sim/people.ts`). A walker idling where a crossing has been laid since, or on the tile it went to gather on with no way home, stands at that crossing's edge on the side of its home's door, and a figure alighting at a platform a road crosses is set down at the platform's edge, where its way off leaves it (`offCrossing` in `src/sim/people.ts`).

Limit: a walker may already be on a crossing when a train is given a way over it: out of a platform, off a siding, after standing with no route, or a new way, round traffic or after a track edit, that its old way did not take. The train sets off from a standstill and may reach the crossing while the walker is still on it. The train's head can stand as far short of the crossing's edge as a train setting off at the hardest pull of any consist (`maxAccel` in `src/data/track.json`, 0.9 tiles/s²) runs while the walker is still on the tile, the step it is given its way among them: 2.87 tiles for a walker going over along the road (a tile at the road pace, 2.5 s), and up to 22.84 tiles for one cutting the crossing corner to corner off the road (`CROSSING_WALK`, 7.07 s). A weaker pull, a top speed or line speed reached before then, or a walker already part way over narrows it. `src/sim/crossings.test.ts` pins both widths.
