21×21 chunks). The author took the recommended options of section 10 with one change: a landmark
appears only where the terrain suits it, and the House Between Seasons where four biomes meet,
and then not always (sections 1 and 2, measured). Epic #529; artwork and handoff #528 (branch
`art/528-rare-landmarks-handoff` @ `210cfe5d`, `docs/art-direction/rare-landmarks-handoff.md` and
`assets/source/rare-landmarks-v1/final/manifest.json` there).

Refreshed by Core on 2026-10-31 against `develop` @ `9c3628c4`, after town stores (#589), the new
UI (#610–#623), the end of gacha and save v23. What changed is listed in section 12. Map generation
has not changed since the sites were measured, so sections 1 and 2 stand.

## Goal

Twelve rare, optional landmarks appear in newly generated terrain. The player spots one, surveys
it by bringing a train near, and then makes a lasting choice: salvage it for a one-time reward, or
restore it with train deliveries for a permanent local benefit. A landmark gives the player a
reason to run a line somewhere; it never gates essential progression.

The handoff describes the landmarks with on-foot exploration, stamina, creatures, combat and
gravity. This game is a railway logistics game, so every one of those is retold as story text in
the landmark's panel and resolved through what the game already has: stations, train deliveries,
the stores, money, passengers, station boosts, weather and the clock (decision D1).

## Non-goals

- No avatar, on-foot exploration, stamina, combat, creatures, gravity or interiors.
- No change to stored terrain: no chunk is regenerated, reshaped, flattened or rerolled, and the
  map generation goldens (`src/world/mapgen.test.ts`) do not change. Landmarks are a layer on top
  of the terrain planes, never part of them.
- No rotation. Landmarks are generated by the world, not built by the player, so each has exactly
  **one painted view** and a fixed footprint orientation. This is unlike player buildings, which
  get four views (textured Blender blockouts plus Codex paint-over). No mirrored or invented views.
- No animation layers, restored or salvaged pictures in the first version (decision D9).
- No balance sign-off: the numbers in section 5 are starting data for play-testing (decision D8).

## 1. The twelve landmarks

Footprint `W×D` is in tiles; `W` runs along the map's +x axis (the lower-left wall in the
picture), `D` along +y (the lower-right wall). The footprints follow the pictures (the author's
decision, 2026-10-23, after an in-game review): each picture is drawn by the game's door rule
(section 8), and its footprint is the ground its base covers at that size, read off the two wall
bases that `measureBase` (`tools/building-fit.mjs`) finds, rounded to whole tiles.

Every landmark has a **site rule**: the terrain it needs around its footprint. "Within r" means in
the footprint grown by `r` tiles on every side; that whole window must lie inside the anchor chunk
(section 2), so the rule reads only tiles generated together. Biomes are read as climates
(`climateAt`), so a river or lake does not hide the climate under it. Fresh water is the `lake` or
`river` biome; the sea does not count.

**Target** is how often the landmark should appear per 3×3-chunk region, as in the handoff.
**Sites** is the share of chunks (start chunk left out) that hold at least one legal footprint
meeting the rule, measured over 40 seeds on 11×11-chunk maps with a 3-tile margin (4 800 chunks;
the script is `2026-10-18-rare-landmarks-sites.measure.ts` next to this file). **Roll** is what
the data must say so that the target comes out (section 2).

| ------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------ | ----- | -------- |
| `merchants_last_stop`     | Merchant's Last Stop    | 2×2       | footprint all plains                          | 6%     | 84.5% | 7.10%    |
| `overgrown_bathhouse`     | Overgrown Bathhouse     | 3×2       | fresh water within 3                          | 5%     | 95.8% | 5.22%    |
| `bellkeepers_refuge`      | Bellkeeper's Refuge     | 1×1       | on a hill (level ≥ 2)                         | 4%     | 64.5% | 6.20%    |
| `flooded_archive`         | Flooded Archive         | 2×2       | fresh water within 2                          | 3%     | 95.8% | 3.13%    |
| `rootbound_workshop`      | Rootbound Workshop      | 1×1       | ≥ 8 forest tiles within 2                     | 3%     | 72.1% | 4.16%    |
| `cliffside_cable_station` | Cliffside Cable Station | 2×2       | a tile 2+ levels higher within 2 (a cliff)    | 2.5%   | 71.3% | 3.51%    |
| `stormglass_observatory`  | Stormglass Observatory  | 1×1       | on a hill (level ≥ 2)                         | 2%     | 64.5% | 3.10%    |

The House Between Seasons' four biomes are its four wings: spring is plains, summer desert, autumn
forest, winter taiga. A desert and a taiga rarely lie close, as one is hot and the other cold, so
a true four-way meeting is rare: with the 3×3 footprint, 0.12% of chunks have one within 3 tiles
(measured over 150 seeds, 18 000 chunks; with the earlier 4×4 footprint it was 0.27%). It is
placed where the meeting is, not in a rolled chunk (section 2), and appears at about one meeting
in four (23%; it was one in ten with the larger footprint).

Neighbouring tiles differ by at most one level (`tileLevels`), so a cliff is a tile two levels up
within two tiles, not a sheer drop at the edge. The cable station's ravine crossing and far
landing are not drawn and need no clearance; its footprint is the station building alone.

## 2. Rarity and placement

All of it is a pure function of the world seed, the world chunk coordinates and the stored tiles
of the chunk being generated, so the same seed gives the same landmarks whatever order the player
expands in.

- **World chunk coordinates.** Map tile `x` lies in world chunk `floor((originX + x) / 32)`
  (`src/world/chunks.ts`); likewise for `y`. These do not move when `expandSave` grows the map.
- **Region.** World chunks are grouped into 3×3 regions: region `(floor(cx / 3), floor(cy / 3))`,
  with floor division for negative coordinates.

There are two ways a landmark is placed. Eleven are **rolled** per region; the House Between
Seasons is **site-led**: it is looked for wherever its site happens to be.

- **Roll.** One roll per region (decision D2): `u = hash(seed, rx, ry, 'landmark')` in `[0, 1)`
  picks a rolled landmark by cumulative bands in data order, each band as wide as the landmark's
  Roll in section 1, or none when `u` is past the last band (the bands add up to 39.6%). The roll
  is wider than the target because the anchor chunk may lack a site; target = roll × sites, so
  each landmark comes out at its target and all eleven together at about 29%. A tuning rule
  `landmarkChance` (0–3, default 1, in `src/sim/rules.ts`) scales every band and the site-led
  chance.
- **Anchor chunk.** A second hash picks one of the region's nine chunks. A rolled landmark can
  appear only there.
- **Site-led.** When a chunk is generated, every footprint in it that meets the House Between
  Seasons' rule is a site. If the chunk has one, `hash(seed, cx, cy, 'house_between_seasons')`
  below 23% places the house on the first site in a seeded order. The 23% comes from the target:
  0.25% per region is 0.028% per chunk, over 0.12% of chunks with a site. If the house and a rolled
  landmark land in the same chunk, the house wins and the rolled one is not placed.
- **Calibration.** The Sites and Roll columns are measured, not exact. A World test re-measures
  the sites on a fixed seed list and fails when roll × sites drifts more than a fifth from the
  target, which is also what a change to map generation would cause. The data then gets new rolls.
- **Spacing.** When two 8-neighbouring regions both roll a landmark and their anchor chunks touch
  (8-neighbour), the one with the lower spacing hash drops out. This only reads rolls, never stored
  state, so it does not depend on order; the nearest two landmarks are then at least one whole
  chunk apart.
- **Which worlds.** Generated worlds only; a level (`spec.kind === 'level'`) has no landmarks.
- **When it is placed.** Only when its anchor chunk is newly generated (the `added` chunks that
  `groundOf` reports while the game starts), and only if the anchor chunk is not the start chunk.
  In a new game that is every chunk of the first map, before the start depot looks for its site,
  so the depot goes round a landmark as it goes round anything the builder refuses. A chunk that is already stored
  never gets a landmark later, so old saves keep their existing land as it is (decision D4).
- **Where.** Up to 64 seeded candidate corners inside the anchor chunk, with the whole footprint at
  least 3 tiles from the chunk's edges (out of reach of `seam()`, and the site rule's window stays
  in the chunk). The first legal candidate wins.
- **Legal** when every footprint tile is in the map, buildable (not water, rock or mountain), at
  one elevation, carries no deposit prop (`coal`, `oil`, `wheat`) and no saved structure (track,
  station, building, decor, road, house), and the landmark's own site rule in section 1 holds.
  Vegetation props on the footprint stay in the chunk store and are simply not drawn.
- **No fit.** If no candidate is legal, the region has no landmark, for good (decision D3).

A landmark is stored as an instance the moment it is placed and is never recomputed on load.

## 3. Stages

| Stage       | Enters when                                                                                        | Player sees                                                                     |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `hidden`    | placed in a chunk that is not revealed                                                             | nothing                                                                         |
| `spotted`   | its chunk is revealed (owned, or 8-neighbour of an owned chunk)                                    | the picture (under fog if unowned) and a `?` marker; panel shows only "Unknown" |
| `surveyed`  | a train stops at a station within reach (decision D5); reach is Chebyshev ≤ 7 tiles from footprint | toast; panel shows the name, story and the paths with their costs and outcomes  |
| `restoring` | the player starts a Restore path                                                                   | panel shows each requirement's delivered/required and any condition             |
| `restored`  | every requirement is delivered and the path's condition holds                                      | toast; restored treatment (section 7); the lasting effect runs from now on      |
| `salvaged`  | the player picks Salvage (from `surveyed`, or from `restoring`)                                    | toast; salvaged treatment; the one-time reward is paid in the same step         |

`restored` and `salvaged` are final. Cancelling a Restore path returns to `surveyed` and keeps no
refund (decision D6). A landmark's tiles block all building, forever; it cannot be demolished
(decision D10).

**Deliveries.** While a landmark is `restoring`, cargo of a required kind unloaded at any station
within reach is credited to it first, up to what is still missing; the rest of the delivery goes on
as today (contracts, then the station's store). Several landmarks within reach of one station are
credited in instance id order. Crediting reads `DeliveryEvent` (`src/sim/trains.ts`) through the
game's `fleet.onDelivery` hook.

A station only unloads what it `accepts` or can store, and a train takes on goods only for a later
stop that takes them, so a landmark's need has to count for both: any station within reach of a
`restoring` landmark takes the cargo the landmark still misses, up to that amount, whatever the
station's own `accepts` says, a depot and a warehouse included. A scheduled train is how goods get
there: a roaming freight train never carries them from a store. The credited goods leave the train
and enter no store.

**Conditions** (decision D11) are checked when the last requirement arrives and then every tick
until they hold: `night` is game hours 20:00–05:00 by `GameClock` (independent of the day/night
display setting); `storm` is wind level `storm` from `windAt` (`src/sim/wind.ts`).

## 4. Paths and outcomes

Every landmark has **Salvage** (instant, one-time reward) and **Restore** (train deliveries,
lasting effect). Two landmarks also make a pick when Restore completes. Outcomes use only these
effect kinds in the first version (decision D7):

| Effect       | What it does                                                                           | Built on                         |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| `grant`      | one-time money and/or cargo, as a recorded transaction                                 | `Economy`, `StoreRegistry`       |
| `produce`    | `n` cargo per game day into a store, optionally consuming `m` of another cargo per day | `tickBuildings`, `worksStore`    |
| `attraction` | `n` passengers per game day more at the nearest passenger station within reach         | `Station` output (`stations.ts`) |
| `boost`      | stations within reach load faster: strength 1 is one decor's `loadBoost` (+0.25)       | `refreshStationBoosts`           |

The global stockpile is gone (town stores, #589), so these say where goods go:

- **`grant`.** Money through `Economy.earn`. Cargo into the nearest store at any distance
  (`StoreRegistry.nearest(x, y, Infinity)`), and what does not fit into the next nearest; what
  fits nowhere is lost. The grant is still paid once.
- **`produce`.** Runs like a works: from the store `supplierFor` gives for the footprint's corner,
  which means its town's store or the nearest within `rules.buildReach`. With no such store, or
  with the consumed cargo missing, it makes nothing that day and the panel says why.
- **`attraction`.** Added to the station's output after its population cap, so a lonely station
  beside a landmark still gets visitors. With no passenger station within reach it adds nothing.
- **`boost`.** Added to the decor boost, outside its limit of two; two landmarks in reach add up.

Phase 2 adds the landmark-specific effects marked "later" below: `reveal` (marks the nearest hidden
landmark as spotted), `forecast` (weather of the next two days in the weather readout), `merchant`
(a recurring contract offer at the site station), `seasonal` (buildings within reach use a chosen
season's `productionMul`) and `recipe` (an optional crafting recipe). Until then each of those
landmarks uses the first-version effect in its row.

## 6. Save format

`SAVE_VERSION` 23 → 24, one new top-level field (a `SaveParts` key, listed in `SAVE_PART_KEYS`).
Track wear and resource modes also want 24: whichever lands later takes the next number.

```
landmarks: {
  nextId: number,
  skipRoll?: true,         // set by the migration; the first load rolls nothing and clears it
  items: Array<{
    id: number,            // unique, from nextId
    def: string,           // landmarks.json id
    x: number, y: number,  // footprint's min corner, map tiles (shifted by expandSave)
    region: [number, number],  // world region, for tests and diagnostics; never shifted
    stage: 'hidden' | 'spotted' | 'surveyed' | 'restoring' | 'restored' | 'salvaged',
    path?: 'salvage' | 'restore',
    pick?: string,         // the Restore pick, where the landmark has one
    delivered?: Record<string, number>,
    stageDay?: number,     // game day the current stage began
    paid?: true,           // the one-time grant was paid; set in the same step that pays it
  }>
}
```

- The step `from: 23` fills `{ nextId: 1, items: [], skipRoll: true }` and nothing else: the
  chunks generated in the first load after the migration are not rolled (a save older than v19
  generates all of its terrain in that load and must not gain landmarks under its track).
- `expandSave` shifts every item's `x`, `y` like other saved coordinates.
- An item whose `def` is unknown is carried through untouched, not drawn and not ticked;
  `pruneUnknownContent` leaves it alone.
- The chunk store and the landmark items are always written in the same snapshot, so a landmark
  can never be lost or doubled by a reload between them; `paid` makes a grant idempotent.

## 7. What the player sees

- **World.** One sprite per landmark in the sortable objects layer, drawn at its footprint, depth
  keyed so that trains in front of and behind it sort correctly. Treatments: `spotted` under the
  existing fog when unowned; `salvaged` darkened and desaturated; `restored` with window lights at
  night (the building lights). Vegetation props under the footprint are not drawn.
- **Marker.** A `?` icon over `spotted` landmarks, in the world and on the overview map.
- **Select tool.** Clicking any footprint tile opens the landmark panel (after station, building
  and decor picks; landmark tiles hold none of those).
- **Tile tooltip.** A landmark tile names the landmark, or "Unknown" while it is `spotted`.
- **Landmark panel.** Built from the new UI kit (`src/ui/kit.ts`), as the other panels. Name and story (or "Unknown" while `spotted`), stage, the two paths with
  what each costs and gives, Start / Salvage / Cancel buttons with a confirmation for the final
  ones, per-cargo progress while `restoring`, the condition and whether it holds, and the effect
  once `restored`.
- **Toasts.** Surveyed, restored, salvaged. A tile notice points to a landmark when it is surveyed.
- **Strings.** Every name, story, path and effect line lives in `src/strings.ts` and, key for key,
  in `src/data/strings.hu.ts`; `landmarkChance` gets its Hungarian label in `src/data/names.hu.ts`.
- **Content editor.** `landmarks.json` is a table of the content editor like the others.
- **Undo.** Start, Salvage and Cancel are not steps of the history: the choices are final.

## 8. Art

- Sources: the twelve PNGs listed in `final/manifest.json` on the art branch; never glob the folder.
  They are illustrated candidates, not projection-certified renders.
- **Size: the game's door rule.** A landmark is drawn like every building
  (`src/render/assetScale.ts`): scaled uniformly so that its door is `DOOR_PX` (13.2 world px).
  The author approved this size on 2026-10-23 after seeing all twelve in a running game beside the
  game's own buildings. It is about 0.62 of the pictures' own 4 m-per-tile scale. The door heights
  used for that review were measured by eye (source px): merchant 135, bathhouse 97, bell tower
  128, workshop 180, cable station 128, observatory 154, conservatory 128, manor 141, foundry 103,
  house 103. The archive (arches, no door) and the pavilion (no door) took the median ratio. Art
  measures them properly and records them, as the buildings' `DOOR_FRACTION` table does.
  The buildings drawn from 3D models went to their real size on 2026-10-27, after this rule; the
  landmarks keep the door rule the author approved for them until the author says otherwise, and
  the review page shows them beside those buildings.
- Art calibrates each one before export: ground-contact corners against the 64×32 grid, the
  footprint's axis, the anchor, the door. A footprint Art measures that differs from section 1 is
  reported to Core, not changed in silence. A picture that does not fit is **regenerated, never
  warped or stretched** (the author's rule).
- Orientation: the pictures' long sides follow section 1's `W` (lower-left wall), except the
  cable station's, whose picture faces the other way; its footprint is square, so only the anchor
  is affected. The inverted manor's base is its upturned roof, so `measureBase` does not read it;
  its 1×1 comes from its body's width.
- Export to a new atlas group `landmarks` (`public/assets/landmarks.{png,json}`), keys
  `landmarks/<id>`, with a procedural placeholder generator in `src/art` so the game runs without
  the files. No existing group or key changes.
- One view per landmark; no `_r<rot>` keys.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

1. For 200 seeds, placement is identical whether the map grows ring by ring or all at once, and
   for any order of the `added` chunk batches (test in `src/world/landmarks.test.ts`).
2. Over 20 000 seeded regions, each rolled landmark is picked within 3 standard errors of its roll
   band, and the house's site-led draw succeeds within 3 standard errors of 23% of sites. On a
   fixed seed list, every landmark's placed rate per region is within a fifth of its target
   (section 1), scaled by `landmarkChance`; this is the calibration test.
3. No placed footprint breaks a legality rule or its site rule, crosses its chunk's 3-tile margin,
   or sits in the start chunk; no two rolled landmarks sit in 8-neighbouring chunks; a house and a
   rolled landmark never share a chunk.
4. A chunk already in the store never gains a landmark: loading a v23 save and expanding it adds
   landmarks only in chunks that were not stored; the mapgen goldens are unchanged.
5. Every save version migrates to 24; a v24 round trip keeps every item; `expandSave` shifts
   items with the rest; a migrated pre-v19 save gets no landmarks in its first load.
6. A one-time grant is paid exactly once across any sequence of save, load, expand and reload, and
   money and cargo change only through recorded transactions.
7. The state machine accepts only the transitions in section 3; deliveries credit at most the
   missing amount; conditions gate completion; cancel keeps no refund. A scheduled train from a
   depot unloads at a plain station within reach of a `restoring` landmark until the landmark has
   what it needs, and takes nothing more there.
8. No build tool can place track, station, building, decor or road on a landmark tile.
9. A review scene shows all twelve on legal terrain at their footprints, with footprint outlines,
   anchors, selection and a train passing in front of and behind each.
10. Essential progression needs no landmark: nothing in `src/data` other than `landmarks.json`
    refers to one.

## 10. Decisions for the author

On 2026-10-22 the author took the recommended option (a) of D1–D11, with one change to D2: a
landmark needs terrain that suits it, and the House Between Seasons appears where four biomes
meet, but not every time. Sections 1 and 2 now say how. The two decisions that change raised were
answered (a) the same day:

- **D12 House Between Seasons' meeting.** (a) plains, desert, forest and taiga all within 3 tiles
  of the footprint (0.27% of chunks), placed at 10% of meetings — recommended; (b) within 6 tiles
  (5.15% of chunks), at 0.5% of meetings; (c) any four of the five land biomes within 3 tiles
  (28% of chunks), at 0.1%.
- **D13 Site rules.** (a) the rules in section 1 as written — recommended; (b) the author changes
  some (any rule changes its measured share and roll, which Core re-measures).

On 2026-10-23, after seeing all twelve in a running game, the author decided:

- **D14 Size and footprints.** Landmarks are drawn by the game's door rule, like the buildings,
  and their footprints follow the pictures at that size (sections 1 and 8).

The original list, answered:

- **D1 Encounter model.** (a) story text + train deliveries + choices in a panel — recommended;
  (b) build avatar, combat, survival systems first.
- **D2 Rolls.** (a) one exclusive roll per 3×3-chunk region, listed chances kept exactly, 29.5% any
  — recommended; (b) twelve independent rolls (a region can get several); (c) one total chance
  plus weighted pick.
- **D3 No fit.** (a) the region has none, for good — recommended; (b) try the other chunks of the
  region (breaks order independence); (c) reshape terrain (forbidden).
- **D4 Where.** (a) only chunks generated from now on, never the start chunk; old saves keep their
  land — recommended; (b) also the start chunk; (c) also scatter into existing saves' land.
- **D5 Discovery.** (a) spotted when the chunk is revealed, surveyed when a train stops within 7
  tiles — recommended; (b) surveyed by clicking and paying a fee; (c) surveyed when the chunk is
  bought.
- **D6 Choices.** (a) Salvage and Restore are final; Restore can be cancelled back, no refund —
  recommended; (b) no cancel once started; (c) cancel with refund.
- **D7 Effects.** (a) first version uses grant, produce, attraction and boost; unique effects
  (reveal, forecast, merchant, seasonal, recipe) in phase 2 — recommended; (b) all unique effects
  in the first version.
- **D8 Numbers.** (a) section 5 is the starting data, tuned by play-testing — recommended; (b) the
  author sets the numbers before implementation.
- **D9 Art states.** (a) one picture for all states, with darkened/lit treatments; restored and
  salvaged pictures later, by Codex — recommended; (b) paint 24 more pictures before release.
- **D10 Demolition.** (a) landmarks can never be removed — recommended; (b) a salvaged one can be
  cleared.
- **D11 Conditions.** (a) Moonwell needs night, Stormglass a storm — recommended; (b) no conditions.
